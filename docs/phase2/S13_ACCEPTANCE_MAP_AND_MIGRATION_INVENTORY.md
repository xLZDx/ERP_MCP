## Migration sequence and safety boundary

The reviewed migration headers declare 004 after 001–003; 005 after 004; 006 after 001–004; 007 after 006; then 008–020 continue in declared predecessor order. Migration 022 explicitly requires the complete 001–021 predecessor chain; migration 023 explicitly requires 001–022; migration 024 explicitly requires 001–023. Do not apply these migrations to an environment missing their declared predecessors.

Migration 022 alone is not an acceptable live activation: its worker-callable `lifecycle_begin_recovery` can leave a scope permanently `RECOVERING`, it does not fence a physical dispatch already admitted before recovery starts, and its fence-row lock waits are unbounded. Local migration 023 revokes the application recovery entry point, makes direct calls fail closed, and bounds lifecycle row-lock waits to five seconds. CR143 records focused disposable PostgreSQL PASS evidence and DB/security/reliability reviews for migration 023. Reliability review found that the timeout setting persisted for the remainder of the transaction; additive migration 024 restores the caller's prior setting after lock acquisition or error. CR145 records functional and E2E PASS on disposable PostgreSQL for migrations 001–024; DB, reliability, and security reviews at exact evidence HEAD `3a5f71d` found no BLOCKER/MAJOR in the changed local scope. DB review qualifies that the savepoint test proves the outer transaction state after rollback but does not isolate restoration performed by the function's exception handler. The reliability review identifies a separate MAJOR acceptance gap before S13 activation: a successful `FOR SHARE` row lock lasts until the transaction ends, and no bounded service transaction lifetime is demonstrated. The physical writer overlap finding remains unresolved because no real writer/service admission integration exists, and no migration exposes a READY transition. Keep 022–024 unapplied outside disposable evidence.
